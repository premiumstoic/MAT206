Water Droplet Nucleation

Classical nucleation theory meets Arrhenius kinetics in a mean-field condensation simulation.

Step 01

Thermodynamics

Saturation pressure follows the Antoine equation. When p > psat, the air is supersaturated:

Δμ = kBT · ln(p / psat)

Volumetric driving force: |Δgv| = nl × |Δμ|

Step 02

Nucleation Theory

Homogeneous: ΔGhom = 16πγ³/(3|Δgv|²), r* = 2γ/|Δgv|

Heterogeneous: f(θ) = (2−3cosθ+cos³θ)/4, ΔGhet = f(θ)×ΔGhom

P = clamp(A · dt · exp(−ΔG / kBT), 0, 1)
Step 03

Growth Kinetics

M(T) = M₀ · exp(−Q / kBT)
Δr = M(T) × |Δgv| × dt

🔬 Exploration Questions

Question 1

Effect of increasing Temperature at fixed vapor pressure

Increasing T exponentially increases psat. Relative humidity drops, Δμ decreases, and the system may cross to undersaturation.
Question 2

Moving from Dry to Tropical Air at fixed Temperature

Higher p increases p/psat, boosting Δμ and |Δgv|, drastically reducing r* and the nucleation barrier.
Question 3

Crossing from Undersaturated to Supersaturated

In undersaturation, Δμ ≤ 0 — droplets evaporate. In supersaturation, Δμ > 0, condensation becomes favorable with a finite critical radius.
Question 4

Effect of Surface Energy (γ) and Contact Angle (θ)

γ: Increases r* linearly and ΔG* as γ³. θ: Low θ (hydrophilic) makes f(θ)→0, nearly eliminating the heterogeneous barrier.
Question 5

Kinetic Parameters: A, M₀, and Q

A: shifts nucleation attempt frequency. M₀: only affects growth rate, not nucleation. Q: suppresses growth exponentially, especially at low T.
Question 6

Favorable Thermodynamics with No Rain?

Yes — high γ prevents nucleation despite favorable Δgv. Or: many droplets nucleate but extremely low M₀ / high Q prevents growth to rain threshold.
Question 7

The "Invisible Droplet" Phenomenon: Why does the Nucleation counter go up, but I don't see any droplets forming?

This simulation spans multiple scales! When a droplet successfully overcomes the energy barrier, it spawns at the critical radius (r*), which is typically on the order of fractions of a nanometer. If your visual scale is 10 nm/pixel, the newly formed droplet is physically smaller than a single pixel. We draw a tiny bright cyan indicator dot to show you where microscopic droplets are hiding. They rely purely on kinetic growth to eventually become macroscopic, visible droplets.
Question 8

How do I make these "invisible" sub-pixel seeds grow fast enough to see them?

You must adjust the kinetic parameters, specifically the Activation Energy (Q). The growth rate is governed by Arrhenius kinetics: dR/dt ∝ exp(−Q/kT). Because Q is inside a negative exponent, decreasing the activation energy causes the droplet mobility to skyrocket exponentially. If Q is too high, the droplets successfully nucleate but are trapped at microscopic sizes. Lowering Q allows them to rapidly bloom into visible rain.
